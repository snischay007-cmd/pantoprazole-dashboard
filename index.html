Pantoprazole drug profile

Approved uses and key label warnings (US FDA Protonix label) [R1, R2].

Approved indications (oral)
  • Short-term treatment of erosive esophagitis associated with GERD
  • Maintenance of healing of erosive esophagitis
  • Pathological hypersecretory conditions including Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (adult dose 40 mg twice daily in the label)
Regulatory label
Warnings and precautions listed
  • Gastric malignancy to be excluded
  • Acute tubulointerstitial nephritis
  • C. difficile-associated diarrhoea
  • Bone fracture (hip, wrist, spine)
  • Severe cutaneous adverse reactions
  • Fundic gland polyps, risk rising beyond 1 year
  • Interference with neuroendocrine tumour work-up and urine THC screens; methotrexate
Regulatory label
Labelled interactions (section headings)
  • Antiretroviral therapy
  • Coumarin anticoagulants
  • Clopidogrel
  • Methotrexate
  • False-positive urine THC
IV formulation
  • Anaphylaxis, thrombophlebitis; consider zinc supplementation if prone to deficiency [R2, 2012 label]
Regulatory label
Off-label uses: not collected in this build. The Indian audits below measured how often use fell outside approved indications, but no trial evidence for specific off-label uses was extracted. CDSCO and Indian Pharmacopoeia labelling were also not retrieved.

Indian real-world evidence

Pooled GERD prevalence in India (95% CI 11.0–20.7), 9 studies, 20,614 subjects; individual studies 5–28.5% and highly heterogeneous. The abstract’s model estimate reads 14.15% (9.9–19.7), so treat the pooled value as approximate [R7]

Hospital prescribing audits

PPI mix, Davangere inpatients (n=216 on PPIs, 2015) [R10]
Each study used a different appropriateness standard: NICE (R8), US FDA indications (R9), National Formulary of India 2011 (R10). Percentages are not directly comparable. In R8, 72% of PPI users received the IV route, and 54% of use was appropriate. A Bengaluru general-medicine abstract (pantoprazole 82.5% of 908 PPI prescriptions) was excluded because the retrieved page marked its abstract as withdrawn.

Esomeprazole 40 mg vs omeprazole 20 mg: odds ratio for healing [R3]

Network meta-analysis of 25 RCTs. Odds ratio above 1 favours esomeprazole. Meta-analysis

Healing-rate ratio vs omeprazole 20 mg, 8 weeks [R4]

Pooled RCTs, 2001. Ratio of 1.0 means equal healing. Pantoprazole’s confidence interval was cut off in the retrieved text. Meta-analysis

Interpretation. Only esomeprazole 40 mg showed a consistent healing advantage over omeprazole 20 mg [R3, R5]. The 2009 comparison found no other PPI significantly better than omeprazole 20 mg, and rabeprazole data were insufficient. Differences in symptom relief, cross-PPI interaction profiles, and branded/generic cost comparisons are unavailable here. The cost study in R9 reported savings from generic substitution but its price table was not extracted.

Methods, limitations and references


Sources were found by web search and read as abstracts or label excerpts. Values were transcribed as printed. Percentages marked “derived” were computed from a printed value. Full texts behind paywalls were not read. Not covered: CDSCO, PvPI, WHO and IPC documents, ClinicalTrials.gov, EMA, dosing details for non-pantoprazole drugs, off-label evidence, and cost data. This page is for research reading and is not clinical advice.
